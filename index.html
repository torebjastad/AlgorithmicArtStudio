🌀 The Mathematics of Algorithmic Art & Generative Physics

1. What is Perlin Noise?

Invented by Ken Perlin in 1983 for the groundbreaking movie Tron (which earned him an Academy Award for Technical Achievement in 1997), Perlin noise is a continuous lattice gradient noise function designed to simulate natural organic textures like clouds, terrain, marble, and turbulent water without the harsh artificial randomness of white noise.

Unlike pure random noise, Perlin noise assigns pseudo-random gradient vectors to integer grid vertices and smoothly interpolates between them using Ken Perlin's quintic curve:

\( s(t) = 6t^5 - 15t^4 + 10t^3 \)

This quintic easing curve has zero 1st and 2nd derivatives at grid boundaries, eliminating visual seams and directional artifacts.

2. Fractional Brownian Motion (fBm) & Octaves

Nature exhibits fractal self-similarity at multiple scales. We achieve this by summing multiple layers (octaves) of noise with doubling frequencies (lacunarity) and halving amplitudes (persistence):

\( \text{fBm}(p) = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \text{persistence}^k \cdot \text{noise}(\text{lacunarity}^k \cdot p) \)

3. Curl Noise: Divergence-Free Flow Fields

Standard potential noise fields cause particles to cluster together into sinks or explode from sources. By computing the mathematical curl \( \nabla \times \vec{\Psi} \) of a scalar noise potential, we obtain a solenoidal (divergence-free) velocity vector field:

\( \vec{v}(x, y) = \left( \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y}, -\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x} \right) \)

Because \( \nabla \cdot (\nabla \times \vec{\Psi}) = 0 \), the fluid is incompressible, producing hypnotic swirling vortices where particles glide endlessly without clumping!

4. Inigo Quilez Domain Warping

Domain warping feeds multi-octave noise back into its own coordinate coordinates:

\( f(p) = \text{fBm}(p + \alpha \cdot \text{fBm}(p + \beta \cdot \text{fBm}(p))) \)

This recursive feedback creates the fluid marbling, liquid smoke, and cosmic nebulae rendered in the GPU GLSL shader mode.
